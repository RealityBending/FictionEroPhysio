1. Physiology

Physiological signals were recorded with a BITalino (ECG, respiration, EDA, photosensor) and EEG with a Muse headband, streamed together to lab recorder and saved as BrainVision files (BIDS format).

ECG (electrocardiography)

Electrodes on the chest record the heart's electrical activity. Each heartbeat produces a characteristic wave whose tallest spike is the R-peak. The time between two R-peaks is the RR interval (or NN interval); heart rate in beats per minute is 60 / RR interval in seconds.

Heart rate is only "measured" once per beat. NeuroKit interpolates between beats so that ECG_Rate has a value at every sample, which means a 2-second window really holds only 2–3 independent values.

What it tells us

Heart rate is controlled by both branches of the autonomic nervous system. Emotional or attention-grabbing images typically cause a brief slowing (orienting response) in the first 1–3 s, followed by a speed-up.

Common problems

  • Movement on the chair or electrodes touching clothing add noise and can create false or missed R-peaks.
  • Unusually large R-peaks or arrhythmias (noted for some participants) affect heart rate and HRV.

Processing: nk.bio_process with NeuroKit defaults: 0.5 Hz high-pass and 50 Hz mains filter, R-peak detection → ECG_Clean, ECG_R_Peaks, ECG_Rate.

Heart rate variability

The heart does not beat like a metronome: RR intervals vary from beat to beat. HRV indices summarise that variation. Higher beat-to-beat variability generally reflects stronger parasympathetic (vagal, "rest and digest") influence.

IndexWhat it measures
MeanNNAverage RR interval (the inverse of mean heart rate)
SDNNStandard deviation of RR intervals: overall variability
RMSSDRoot mean square of successive differences: fast, beat-to-beat variability; the most common vagal index
HFPower in the high-frequency band (0.15–0.4 Hz), linked to breathing and vagal activity
LFHFRatio of low- to high-frequency power (interpretation as "sympathovagal balance" is disputed)
SampEn, HFDComplexity / irregularity of the RR series
IALS, Ca, AIHeart rate asymmetry: whether accelerations and decelerations contribute differently

Guidelines (Task Force, 1996) recommend at least ~5 minutes for short-term HRV. The 8-minute resting state meets this; the 20–45 s HCT intervals only allow time-domain "ultra-short" HRV.

Respiration (RSP)

A belt around the lower chest stretches with each breath, giving one peak per breath. From it we get breathing rate (breaths per minute) and amplitude (depth).

Breathing changes with arousal and directly affects heart rate: heart rate rises on the in-breath and falls on the out-breath (respiratory sinus arrhythmia).

Common problems

  • A belt that is too tight or too loose flattens the signal (several session notes mention adjusting it).
  • Talking, yawning, sighing and moving distort single breaths; asthma can make the wave jagged.

Processing: NeuroKit defaults, filtered to the breathing range (about 0.05–3 Hz), breaths detected → RSP_Rate, RSP_Amplitude.

EDA (electrodermal activity, skin conductance)

Two electrodes on the fingers measure how well the skin conducts a tiny current (microsiemens, µS). Sweat glands there are controlled only by the sympathetic ("fight or flight") nervous system, so EDA is a fairly pure marker of arousal.

  • Tonic (skin conductance level): the slow background level, drifting over minutes.
  • Phasic (skin conductance responses, SCRs): fast bumps starting 1–3 s after a stimulus and peaking a few seconds later.

Common problems

  • Non-responders: cold hands or poor circulation give a flat signal (checked with the double-sniff and pinch tests).
  • Gel drying gives a steadily falling signal; extra isotonic gel was added at the break when needed.
  • The BITalino sensor saturates ("clips") at about 25 µS.

Recorded in: the Fiction task only (analysed). Processing: smoothed and split into EDA_Tonic and EDA_Phasic (NeuroKit default high-pass method), SCR peaks detected.

Photosensor (PHOTO channel)

The experiment draws a black square (100 × 100 px, top-left corner of the screen) whenever an important event is on screen, and a light sensor taped over that corner records it. The PHOTO signal drops when the square is black, so it marks exactly when something was displayed, not just when the computer asked for it. Between events the square is white. Every marker is also sent as an LSL marker ("1" on, "0" off).

Events, epochs and baseline

  • Event: a dark period on the PHOTO channel (onset and duration), found with nk.events_find(..., threshold_keep="below") and a minimum duration that fits the task.
  • Epoch: a window of signal cut around an event (e.g. 2 s before to 5 s after an image), with time 0 = the event.
  • Baseline correction: subtracting a reference value so each trial's response is measured from its own starting level. This study uses the value at image onset.

What the black square marks in each task is described under Tasks.

Sampling rate

The number of measurements per second (Hz). The BITalino records at 1000 Hz and the Muse EEG at 256 Hz. When the streams were combined into one file, everything was resampled to a shared 2000 Hz timeline, so 1 second = 2000 samples. The scripts write durations as srate * seconds to convert seconds into samples.

The devices stream independently, so a crash of one device (e.g. "Bitalino crashed, reconnected" in the notes) shows up as a gap of missing values in its channels only.

2. Tasks

The session runs as three separate experiment files, each saving its own data: RestingState.html (resting state), Interoception.html (TAP, then HCT and questionnaires in random order) and fiction.html (Fiction). Each task has its own physiological recording (sub-XXX_task-RS/TAP/HCT/FIC_run-001_eeg.vhdr) and, apart from the resting state, a behavioural file (sub-XXX_task-TAP/HCT/FIC_beh.tsv).

Design

8 minutes of rest, eyes closed, without falling asleep (RestingState.js).

Grey screen, 1 s→ Fixation cross, 8 min→ Beep + Continue→ Questionnaire

Questionnaire (ARSQ-style): 21 items about what went through their mind, 3 per dimension: DoM (discontinuity of mind), ToM (theory of mind), Self, Plan, Sleep, Comfort, SomA (somatic awareness), named e.g. DoM_1–DoM_3. Shuffled order, one page, rated 0–6 (Completely Disagree → Completely Agree).

Photosensor

Black for the whole 8-minute fixation period (one long event). White during the 1 s grey screen before it.

Data coding

  • No behavioural file; the questionnaire answers are added to the participant row (one column per item).
  • Physiology (1_physio.py): ECG and RSP; recording cropped to the 8-min marker (events ≥ 5 s); nk.bio_process; 10 HRV indices from all R-peaks, named with _RS: HRV_MeanNN_RS, HRV_SDNN_RS, HRV_RMSSD_RS, HRV_SampEn_RS, HRV_HF_RS, HRV_HFD_RS, HRV_LFHF_RS, HRV_IALS_RS, HRV_Ca_RS, HRV_AI_RS → rawdata_participants.csv.
  • QC plots: signals/rs_ecg_all.png, signals/rs_rsp_all.png.

Design

Two families of tapping tasks (TAP.js), all responses with the spacebar. A 3-2-1 countdown (3 s) precedes every block except Heart.

Voluntary tapping (clock): a rotating hand sweeps around a circle in one full revolution of 4–6 s (random).

  • External: press when the hand reaches a red target arrow (placed 0.5–0.95 of a revolution after the start).
  • Internal: press at a self-chosen moment before the sweep ends.
  • Blocks: 40 External → 40 Internal → 80 Mixed (half each, shuffled; the circle is green for External and blue for Internal). One press per trial; a warning appears if no press.

Rhythmic tapping: 120 taps each.

  • Synchrony: 10 beeps at about 42 per minute (1.43 s apart) to tap along with, then 120 taps keeping that rhythm.
  • Random: tap at random, irregular intervals.
  • Heart: tap at each felt heartbeat.

Photosensor

  • Clock trials: black from trial start until the spacebar press (about 0.5–6 s), or until the trial ends if there was no press.
  • Rhythmic trials: white while waiting; black for 60 ms after each tap.
The 60 ms markers are only 3–4 screen frames, so they may be weak or missed by the sensor. load_tap therefore uses the photosensor only to crop the recording (events ≥ 0.05 s); the timing of individual taps should come from the behavioural file.

Design

Heartbeat Counting Task (Schandry, 1981; HCT.js): silently count heartbeats without touching the body, from "3-2-1" until a beep.

Countdown 3 s→ Fixation: counting interval→ Beep + Continue→ Count + confidence
  • 6 intervals of 20, 25, 30, 35, 40 and 45 s, in random order.
  • After each: count (number, under 100) and confidence (0 = Not confident → 5 = Very confident).
  • Run in Interoception.html after TAP; the HCT and the questionnaires (MAIA, MINT, IAS) come in random order.

Photosensor

Black for the whole counting interval (20–45 s). White during the countdown.

Physiology (1_physio.py): 6 intervals found on the photosensor (≥ 10 s, must be exactly 6); R-peaks counted in each.

  • HCT_Accuracy = mean over intervals of 1 − |count − R-peaks| / R-peaks
  • HCT_Sensibility = mean confidence
  • HCT_Awareness = Spearman correlation between confidence and accuracy (Garfinkel et al., 2015)
  • HRV_MeanNN_HCT, HRV_SDNN_HCT, HRV_RMSSD_HCT: time-domain HRV per interval, averaged

→ rawdata_participants.csv; QC plots signals/hct_ecg_all.png, signals/hct_rsp_all.png.

Design

Images from NAPS-ERO presented as either real photographs or AI-generated (fiction.js). Cover story: a GAN developed by a Sussex / AI-startup partnership.

  • Stimuli: 125 images (25 each: Female, Male, Female Couple, Male Couple, Opposite-sex Couple). The 2 categories matching the participant's gender/sexual orientation (or their choice) are kept: about 50 images.
  • Condition: within each category, half labelled Reality ("Photograph") and half Fiction ("AI-generated"), randomly per participant. Each label has its own random cue colour (2 of red / blue / green).

Phase 1 (physiology recorded), split in two halves with a break and eye-tracker recalibration:

Fixation 3 s→ Label 2 s→ Fixation 3–5 s→ Image 4 s→ Ratings

Ratings: Body_Reaction (slider 0–100), Valence (0–6, negative → positive), Enticement (0–6, not at all → very much), plus an attention check on the label.

Phase 2 (no physiology): told the labels were mixed up; each image shown again for 1 s and rated on Realness (−100 AI-generated → +100 Photograph).

Photosensor

Black during each image: 4 s in Phase 1 (1 s in Phase 2, not recorded). The label is not marked.

→ rawdata_trials.csv (one row per image), rawdata_hr_timecourse.csv and signals/fic_hr_timecourse.png; QC plots signals/fic_ecg_all.png, fic_rsp_all.png, fic_eda_all.png.

If a recording was restarted (several FIC_run-00X files), load_fiction joins the runs in order.
